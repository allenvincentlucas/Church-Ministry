Small Enough to Miss, Large Enough to Shelter

A seed you could lose in your palm and a pinch of yeast you'd never notice both become impossible to miss once they've finished their work.

Matthew 13:31–35

🌱 The Reading

Another parable he proposed unto them, saying: The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and sowed in his field.

Which is the least indeed of all seeds; but when it is grown up, it is greater than all herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come, and dwell in the branches thereof.

Another parable he spoke to them: The kingdom of heaven is like to leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.

All these things Jesus spoke in parables to the multitudes: and without parables he did not speak to them.

That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying: I will open my mouth in parables, I will utter things hidden from the foundation of the world.

Matthew 13:31–35, Douay-Rheims (Challoner)

🍞 Reflection

Jesus gives us two pictures of the kingdom of heaven in the same breath, and neither one is impressive to look at. A mustard seed is so small you could lose it in the crease of your palm. A pinch of yeast disappears the moment it's folded into the dough — you couldn't point to it if you tried. And yet Jesus says: this is what the kingdom is like. Not a monument. Not an announcement. Something started so quietly that most people would walk right past it, that ends up sheltering birds in its branches or leavening an entire batch of bread.

We tend to assume that things which matter should look like they matter from the outset. If the mustard seed doesn't look like a tree yet, we figure it probably isn't going to become one. If the yeast isn't visibly doing anything, we assume nothing is happening. Jesus is telling us that's exactly backwards — that the kingdom's power is precisely in how unremarkable its beginnings are, and in how completely they work once given time.

Picture this: A new hire joins a team that's been worn down by a few too many broken promises and missed follow-ups. She doesn't try to fix the culture. She just does one small thing consistently: she remembers what people told her last week, and she follows through on the small things she says she'll do. Six months in, nobody can point to the exact day anything changed. But somewhere along the way, people started trusting each other's word again — not because of a new policy, but because one person quietly, repeatedly, did the unglamorous thing. That's yeast working through the dough. Nobody saw it happen. Everyone can taste the difference now.

What's the small, unglamorous thing you already do — or could start doing — that you're tempted to think doesn't matter because it's too small for anyone to notice?

📖 Catechism Cross-References

CCC 541

The Church herself is described as "the seed and beginning" of the Kingdom of God on earth — Christ gathers people around himself the way that first, hidden planting gathers everything that grows from it.

Read CCC 541 at vatican.va →
CCC 543

The word of the Lord is compared directly to a seed sown in a field: those who receive it in faith have already received the kingdom, and from there it grows by its own power, without needing to be forced, until the harvest.

Read CCC 543 at vatican.va →
CCC 764

The Kingdom doesn't arrive all at once in full visibility — it shines out gradually in Christ's word, his works, and his presence, beginning with the small, ordinary gathering of those who chose to follow him.

Read CCC 764 at scborromeo.org →

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, so much of what I do feels too small to matter. Help me remember that you don't need me to be a tree today — only a seed willing to be planted. Work through the ordinary, unnoticed things I do this week the way yeast works through dough: quietly, completely, until the difference is unmistakable. Amen.

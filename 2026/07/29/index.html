Ten minutes with today's Gospel

Right Here, Not Someday

Martha already believed in the resurrection at the end of time. Jesus asks her to believe in him, standing in front of her, right now.

John 11:19-27

📖 Today's Gospel

And many of the Jews were come to Martha and Mary, to comfort them concerning their brother.

Martha therefore, as soon as she heard that Jesus had come, went to meet him: but Mary sat at home.

Martha therefore said to Jesus: Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.

But now also I know that whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee.

Jesus saith to her: Thy brother shall rise again.

Martha saith to him: I know that he shall rise again, in the resurrection at the last day.

Jesus said to her: I am the resurrection and the life: he that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live:

And every one that liveth, and believeth in me, shall not die for ever. Believest thou this?

She saith to him: Yea, Lord, I have believed that thou art Christ the Son of the living God, who art come into this world.

❤ Reflection

Martha meets Jesus on the road before he even reaches the house. She isn't hiding her grief, and she isn't hiding her faith either — in almost the same breath she says "my brother would not have died" and "I know he will rise on the last day." She's got the whole thing memorized. She believes the doctrine. What she isn't ready for is Jesus turning that doctrine into a face-to-face question: not "do you believe in the resurrection," but "do you believe in me — right now, standing in front of you?"

That's the turn in this passage. Martha's faith was aimed at some future day, a safe distance from her actual grief. Jesus doesn't correct her theology. He relocates it. The resurrection isn't a date on a calendar to wait out — it's a person, already there, already asking to be trusted with the exact wound she's carrying today.

Picture this: you lost someone eighteen months ago, and you still haven't deleted their name from your phone. You believe, in a general and comforting way, that you'll see them again — heaven, resurrection, all of it. But most days that belief lives far away from the actual ache: the empty chair, the birthday you still mark on the calendar, the voicemail you can't bring yourself to erase. It's true. It's just distant. It doesn't quite reach today.

Where is your faith parked at "someday" — and what would it look like to let Jesus ask you about it today instead?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 994

The Church teaches that Jesus ties the hope of resurrection to his own identity, not only to a future event — because he already raises some of the dead now and will raise every believer on the last day, faith in the resurrection is really faith in him.

Read CCC 994 →
CCC 166

Faith isn't just an inherited fact you passively hold. The Catechism calls it a personal act — each person's own free response when God reveals himself — even though that same faith is also carried and supported by the whole believing community.

Read CCC 166 →
CCC 150

Faith has two inseparable parts: giving yourself personally to God, and freely assenting to everything he has revealed as true. Martha does both at once when she answers Jesus.

Read CCC 150 →

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, I believe you are the resurrection and the life — not just for some far-off day, but for me, right here, in whatever I'm grieving or waiting on right now. When my faith drifts into a safe, distant someday, bring it back into the room with you. Ask me the question Martha heard, and give me the grace to answer the way she did: yes, Lord, I believe. Amen.

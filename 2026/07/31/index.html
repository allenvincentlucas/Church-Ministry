Too Familiar to Believe

The people who knew Jesus longest were the ones who could see him least. Nazareth's certainty about who he was closed the door on what he wanted to do there.

Matthew 13:54-58

📖 Today's Gospel

And coming into his own country, he taught them in their synagogues, so that they wondered and said: How came this man by this wisdom and miracles?

Is not this the carpenter's son? Is not his mother called Mary, and his brethren James, and Joseph, and Simon, and Jude:

And his sisters, are they not all with us? Whence therefore hath he all these things?

And they were scandalized in his regard. But Jesus said to them: A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house.

And he wrought not many miracles there, because of their unbelief.

❤ Reflection

By the time Jesus stands up in the Nazareth synagogue, everyone in the room has known him for thirty years. They know his mother, his brothers, his sisters, his father's trade. That kind of familiarity is usually a gift — it's how most of our closest relationships work. Here, it works against him. They can't hear the wisdom because they've already decided who he is: the carpenter's son, not a prophet.

Matthew doesn't say Jesus couldn't work miracles there. He says Jesus "wrought not many miracles there, because of their unbelief." Their certainty about him became a kind of ceiling — not because God's power ran out, but because there was nowhere for it to land. Faith isn't only believing the right things about Jesus; it's staying open enough that he can still surprise you, even in the people and places you're sure you've already figured out.

Picture this: a sibling who used to be reckless, and you still brace for the reckless version even though they've quietly changed. A friend from a hard season you still see through that old lens. Or maybe it's yourself — you've decided you already know exactly what you're capable of, and you've quietly stopped expecting God to do anything new through you.

Who in your life have you already decided you understand completely — and what would it look like to let Jesus surprise you there instead?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 439

Many people recognized real messianic attributes in Jesus, but some understood it in too human, too political a sense. Nazareth's problem was similar in miniature: they had a category ready for "carpenter's son" and no category ready for "prophet" — so they fit him into the smaller box they already had.

Read CCC 439 →
CCC 153

Believing in Jesus is described as more than intellectual agreement with facts about him — it's a grace-given act of trusting the whole person of Christ, which requires the help of the Holy Spirit to move beyond appearances into the truth of who he is.

Read CCC 153 →
CCC 2717

Contemplative prayer is described as a gaze of faith fixed on Jesus — the opposite of Nazareth's gaze, which looked at him and saw only the carpenter's son. Learning to see Christ freshly is itself a form of prayer.

Read CCC 2717 →

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, forgive me for the times I've decided I already know someone — you included. Loosen my grip on the story I've written about the people closest to me. Give me the kind of faith that stays open to surprise, even in familiar places, even in ordinary people, even in myself. Amen.

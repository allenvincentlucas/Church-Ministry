Why God Waits for the Harvest

Jesus explains the weeds among the wheat: judgment is certain, but it belongs to the harvest, not to today.

Matthew 13:36–43

🌾 The Reading

Then having sent away the multitudes, he came into the house, and his disciples came to him, saying: Expound to us the parable of the cockle of the field.

Who made answer and said to them: He that soweth the good seed, is the Son of man.

And the field, is the world. And the good seed are the children of the kingdom. And the cockle, are the children of the wicked one.

And the enemy that sowed them, is the devil. But the harvest is the end of the world. And the reapers are the angels.

Even as cockle therefore is gathered up, and burnt with fire: so shall it be at the end of the world.

The Son of man shall send his angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all scandals, and them that work iniquity.

And shall cast them into the furnace of fire. There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

Then shall the just shine as the sun, in the kingdom of their Father. He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.

Matthew 13:36–43, Douay-Rheims (Challoner)

This page uses the Douay-Rheims (Challoner revision) translation because it's in the public domain and safe to reproduce in full — note it renders the plant as "cockle" where the Mass reading's NABRE translation says "weeds" (both refer to darnel, a weed that closely resembles young wheat). Read the official NABRE text at USCCB's Daily Readings. If you were hoping for another modern translation (RSVCE, NIV, etc.), those are also copyrighted and aren't reproduced here either.

⏳ Reflection

When the disciples finally get Jesus alone, they ask him to explain the parable he'd just told the crowd — and he does, plainly, almost like a key: the sower is the Son of Man, the field is the world, the good seed and the weeds are two kinds of people growing up side by side, and the harvest is the end of the age. It's one of the few times Jesus explains a parable point by point instead of leaving it to sit with us. But the detail worth slowing down on isn't the fire at the end. It's the waiting in the middle. Nothing gets pulled up early. The weeds stay right next to the wheat, on purpose, because uprooting one this soon would damage the other.

That's not indifference from God. It's protection. He is not slow to deal with evil because he doesn't notice it — he's patient because the field is still growing, and premature judgment would cost the wheat too. The line between "the children of the kingdom" and "the children of the evil one" isn't always as visible as we'd like it to be, least of all inside our own hearts. God's timeline gives more of us more time.

Picture this: A mother watches her grown son drift further from the faith she raised him in. Every visit home, she feels the pull to force it — one more conversation to fix it, one more argument to win, one more ultimatum to finally make him see. She could reach for that. Instead, she chooses to keep showing up: dinner on the table, no lectures, prayers said quietly where he can't hear them. It isn't resignation. It's trusting that "not yet" is not the same word as "never," and that some things aren't hers to force into bloom before their season.

Where are you tempted to force a resolution today that isn't yours to force — a harvest that isn't ready yet?

📖 Catechism Cross-References

CCC 681

On the last day, Christ will come in glory to bring about the final triumph of good over evil — the same good and evil that, like the wheat and the weeds in today's parable, have grown up side by side throughout history.

Read CCC 681 at vatican.va →
CCC 1039

At the Last Judgment, everything hidden comes into the light: the true condition of each person's relationship with God, including the good that was done and the good that was left undone.

Read CCC 1039 at vatican.va →
CCC 1040

The point of that final reckoning isn't fear for its own sake — it's the promise that God's justice will finally triumph over every injustice, and that his love proves stronger, in the end, than death itself.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, give me the patience you have with the field. Keep me from ripping up too soon what you're still tending — in others, and in the parts of my own heart still tangled between wheat and weed. I trust you with the harvest. Let me trust you with the waiting, too. Amen.

Not Yours to Sort Yet

The good and the bad stay in the same net until the very end. Jesus doesn't ask us to sort the catch — only to keep bringing out both old wisdom and new.

Matthew 13:47-53

📖 Today's Gospel

Again the kingdom of heaven is like to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of all kind of fishes.

Which, when it was filled, they drew out, and sitting by the shore, they chose out the good into vessels, but the bad they cast forth.

So shall it be at the end of the world. The angels shall go out, and shall separate the wicked from among the just.

And shall cast them into the furnace of fire: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

Have ye understood all these things? They say to him: Yes.

He said unto them: Therefore every scribe instructed in the kingdom of heaven, is like to a man that is a householder, who bringeth forth out of his treasure new things and old.

And it came to pass: when Jesus had finished these parables, he passed from thence.

❤ Reflection

Jesus ends this long day of parables with two quick, back-to-back pictures: a fishing net that hauls in everything, good and bad together, and a scribe who knows how to reach into a full storeroom and bring out both old treasure and new. Neither picture asks us to sort people out today. The sorting in the net parable happens "at the end of the age" — not now, and not by us. What happens now is quieter: staying in the net, and learning to draw on everything you've been given, old and new alike.

That's easy to miss if all you remember is the scary part — the furnace, the weeping and grinding of teeth. But look at what comes right after it. Jesus doesn't leave the disciples with dread; he asks them a simple, personal question: "Have you understood all these things?" And when they say yes, he gives them one more job. Not judge. Steward — someone who keeps bringing out both the new and the old.

Picture this: a small group, a parish committee, or your extended family group chat. It's a mixed catch — some people show up faithfully, some drift in and out, some conversations build you up and some just drain you. It's tempting to want to prune the list down to the good ones and let the rest go. Jesus doesn't hand you that job today. He asks for something smaller and steadier: keep showing up with what you've long known to be true, and stay open to whatever new thing he's still teaching you through the very people you'd rather sort out.

Where have you been trying to do the angels' sorting work early — and what would it look like to set that job down?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 827

The Church herself is described as holy and always in need of purification — the weeds of sin stay mixed with the good wheat of the Gospel in every one of us until the end of time. The net parable's mixed catch isn't just "out there"; it runs through each of us too.

Read CCC 827 →
CCC 1039

At the Last Judgment, every person's true relationship with God will be laid bare in the full light of Christ. The sorting the net parable describes isn't guesswork or gossip — it's a final clarity that belongs to Christ alone, in his own time.

Read CCC 1039 →
CCC 94

The Church's understanding of God's revelation keeps growing over time, through prayerful reflection, study, and the Church's ongoing teaching — a living echo of the scribe who draws out both old treasure and new.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, I don't have to sort the field or the net today — that isn't mine to do. Teach me instead to be a good steward of what you've given me: old wisdom I already trust, and new things you're still showing me. Give me patience for a harvest that isn't in my hands, and let me stay faithfully in the net until you bring it in. Amen.

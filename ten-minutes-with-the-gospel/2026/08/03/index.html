Little Faith Is Still Enough

Peter was doing fine walking on water — until he looked at the wind instead of at Jesus. The moment he started sinking is also the moment Jesus was already reaching for him.

Matthew 14:22-36

📖 Today's Gospel

And forthwith Jesus obliged his disciples to go up into the boat, and to go before him over the water, till he dismissed the people. And having dismissed the multitude, he went into a mountain alone to pray. And when it was evening, he was there alone.

But the boat in the midst of the sea was tossed with the waves: for the wind was contrary. And in the fourth watch of the night, he came to them walking upon the sea. And they seeing him walk upon the sea, were troubled, saying: It is an apparition. And they cried out for fear. And immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying: Be of good heart: it is I, fear ye not.

And Peter making answer, said: Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters. And he said: Come. And Peter going down out of the boat, walked upon the water to come to Jesus. But seeing the wind strong, he was afraid: and when he began to sink, he cried out, saying: Lord, save me. And immediately Jesus stretching forth his hand took hold of him, and said to him: O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?

And when they were come up into the boat, the wind ceased. And they that were in the boat came and adored him, saying: Indeed thou art the Son of God.

And having passed the water, they came into the country of Genesar. And when the men of that place had knowledge of him, they sent into all that country, and brought to him all that were diseased. And they besought him that they might touch but the hem of his garment. And as many as touched, were made whole.

❤ Reflection

After a full day feeding thousands, Jesus sends the disciples ahead and finally gets what he needed all along — solitude, on a mountain, to pray. Hours later, in the middle of a rough crossing, he comes toward the boat walking on the sea. Their first reaction isn't relief. It's terror. Fear can distort the sight of help arriving, make it look like a threat instead.

Peter's request is bold: "bid me come to thee upon the waters." And it works — for a moment, he is actually walking on water. Then he notices the wind. He gets scared, and he starts to sink. What's easy to miss is what happens next: Jesus doesn't wait for Peter to calm down or get his nerve back. He reaches out immediately, before a word of correction. "Why didst thou doubt" comes after the rescue, not as a condition for it.

Picture this: you finally work up the courage for something hard — a difficult conversation, a decision you've been putting off, forgiving someone who hurt you. You're doing fine, actually moving through it. Then a setback, a doubt, or someone else's skepticism becomes "the wind," and suddenly you notice how exposed you are. The instinct is to assume you've blown it. But the hand reaching for you doesn't wait for your faith to be perfect first.

What's the "wind" that makes you doubt partway through something you already had the courage to start — and have you noticed Jesus doesn't wait for your faith to be flawless before reaching for you?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 2602

This paragraph names today's exact scene: Jesus often drew apart to pray in solitude, on a mountain, preferably at night. His visible works — like walking out to the boat — are described as the manifestation of that hidden prayer.

Read CCC 2602 →
CCC 162

Faith is described as a free gift that can be lost if it isn't nourished — we have to "beg the Lord to increase" it. Peter's faith wasn't fake because it wavered; it was real and small, exactly the kind that needs tending.

Read CCC 162 →
CCC 442

Recognizing Jesus as the Son of God is described as something "flesh and blood" cannot arrive at alone — it takes revelation. The disciples' declaration in the boat, "Indeed thou art the Son of God," is a moment of exactly that grace breaking through.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, when I start to sink partway through trusting you, don't wait for me to have it all figured out — reach for me the way you reached for Peter. Keep my eyes on you, not on the wind. Amen.

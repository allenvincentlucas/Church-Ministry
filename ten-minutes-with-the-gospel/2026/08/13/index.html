Ten minutes with today's Gospel

Stop Counting

Peter's offer of seven times was already generous. Jesus's answer was never about hitting a bigger number — it was about ending the count altogether.

Matthew 18:21 – 19:1

📖 Today's Gospel

Then came Peter unto him and said: Lord, how often shall my brother offend against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith to him: I say not to thee, till seven times; but till seventy times seven times.

Therefore is the kingdom of heaven likened to a king, who would take an account of his servants. And when he had begun to take the account, one was brought to him, that owed him ten thousand talents. And as he had not wherewith to pay it, his lord commanded that he should be sold, and his wife and children, and all that he had, and payment to be made. But that servant falling down, besought him, saying: Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all. And the lord of that servant being moved with pity, let him go, and forgave him the debt.

But when that servant was gone out, he found one of his fellow servants that owed him an hundred pence: and laying hold of him, he throttled him, saying: Pay what thou owest. And his fellow servant falling down, besought him, saying: Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all. And he would not: but went and cast him into prison, till he paid the debt. Now his fellow servants seeing what was done, were very much grieved, and they came and told their lord all that was done. Then his lord called him; and said to him: Thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all the debt, because thou besoughtest me: Shouldst not thou then have had compassion also on thy fellow servant, even as I had compassion on thee? And his lord being angry, delivered him to the torturers until he paid all the debt. So also shall my heavenly Father do to you, if you forgive not every one his brother from your hearts.

❤ Reflection

Peter's proposed number — seven — wasn't stingy. In his culture, seven represented completeness; offering to forgive someone seven times over already sounded generous, maybe even a little heroic. Jesus's answer isn't a bigger number to hit and then be finished. "Seventy times seven" isn't a higher ceiling — it's a way of saying the counting itself was always the problem.

The parable that follows shows why. A servant is forgiven an impossibly large debt — years and years of wages, unpayable by any honest means — and then turns around and chokes a fellow servant over a debt that's comparatively tiny. The scandal isn't that he was owed something real. It's that he'd just been forgiven something enormously larger and immediately went back to keeping score. Real forgiveness received changes the math for what we think we're owed by others — or it should.

Picture this: a running mental tally on someone close to you — a spouse, a family member, a coworker — every offense logged and ready to be cited. "This is the fifth time." The ledger feels justified in the moment. This Gospel doesn't ask you to pretend the offenses didn't happen. It asks whether you've actually let the count go, the way you've been let go of.

Whose debt are you still counting — and what would it look like to actually let the number go, the way you've been forgiven?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 2843

"Now — and this is daunting — this outpouring of mercy cannot penetrate our hearts as long as we have not forgiven those who have trespassed against us." The Catechism ties our own capacity to receive mercy directly to our willingness to extend it — exactly the logic of today's parable.

Read CCC 2843 →
CCC 2838-2839

On the petition "forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us" in the Lord's Prayer — the Catechism notes this is the one petition where Jesus makes our own forgiveness of others part of the very words we're asking God to hear.

Read CCC 2838-2839 →
CCC 982

"There is no offense, however serious, that the Church cannot forgive... Christ who died for all men desires that in his Church the gates of forgiveness should always be open to anyone who turns away from sin." No offense is placed outside the reach of the mercy this parable describes.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, show me the ledger I've been keeping on someone, and give me the grace to close it — not because the hurt wasn't real, but because I've been forgiven a debt far larger than any I'm owed. Teach me to stop counting. Amen.

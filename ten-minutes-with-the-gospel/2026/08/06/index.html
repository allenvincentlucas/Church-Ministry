Not a Place to Build a Tent

Peter wants to stay on the mountain and build three tents. Before he even finishes speaking, the cloud interrupts him — the glimpse of glory was never meant to be a new address.

Matthew 17:1-9

📖 Today's Gospel

And after six days Jesus taketh unto him Peter and James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into a high mountain apart: And he was transfigured before them. And his face did shine as the sun: and his garments became white as snow.

And behold there appeared to them Moses and Elias talking with him. And Peter answering, said to Jesus: Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles, one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias.

And as he was yet speaking, behold a bright cloud overshadowed them. And lo, a voice out of the cloud, saying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased: hear ye him. And the disciples hearing, fell upon their face, and were very much afraid.

And Jesus came and touched them: and said to them, Arise, and fear not. And they lifting up their eyes, saw no one, but only Jesus. And as they came down from the mountain, Jesus charged them, saying: Tell the vision to no man, till the Son of man be risen from the dead.

❤ Reflection

Peter's instinct is completely understandable. He's just seen something no one else has ever seen — Jesus transfigured, Moses and Elijah standing right there, glory breaking into the ordinary world in a way that can't be unseen. Of course he wants to stay. "Lord, it is good that we are here. If you wish, I will make three tents." Why would anyone want to leave this and go back down to ordinary life?

But the text doesn't even let him finish the thought. "While he was still speaking" — mid-sentence — the cloud overshadows them, and the voice reorients the entire moment: not "stay here," but "listen to him." This happens right after Jesus first told the disciples, in the previous chapter, that he would suffer and die in Jerusalem. The mountain wasn't a new home to build. It was something to carry back down — a glimpse strong enough to hold onto through the road that was still coming.

Picture this: a retreat weekend, a moment of prayer that felt effortless, a season where God's presence was almost tangible. And then it ends, and ordinary life — bills, arguments, the same slow struggles — comes rushing back in, and it can feel like a letdown by comparison. The mountain wasn't a mistake to leave. It was never meant to be lived on. It was meant to be carried.

Have you ever had a mountaintop moment you wanted to hold onto or rebuild, instead of carrying its strength back down into ordinary life? What would "listen to him" look like there instead?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 554-556

The Catechism connects the Transfiguration directly to what comes just before it in the Gospel — Jesus's first prediction of his own suffering and death. The glory on the mountain is described as strengthening the disciples for the scandal of the cross still to come, not as a replacement for it.

Read CCC 554-556 →
CCC 2583

The Transfiguration is named as a "moment of prayer" — Jesus praying on the mountain — that becomes the setting for the deepest revelation of who he is. Prayer, in the Catechism's telling, is where his glory becomes visible, not a separate thing from it.

Read CCC 2583 →
CCC 1090

The Church's own liturgy is described as offering "a foretaste of the heavenly liturgy" already glimpsed in moments like this one — a preview meant to be carried into ordinary worship and ordinary life, not confined to the mountaintop where it first appeared.

Read CCC 1090 →

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, when you give me a glimpse of your glory, keep me from trying to build a permanent home out of the feeling. Help me carry it back down the mountain, into the ordinary road still ahead, and to listen to you there. Amen.

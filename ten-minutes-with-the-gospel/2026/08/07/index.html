The Losing That Finds You

Whoever tries to save his life will lose it. Whoever loses it for my sake will find it. Jesus isn't describing a fair trade — he's naming a paradox about what we grip too tightly.

Matthew 16:24-28

📖 Today's Gospel

Then Jesus said to his disciples: If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For he that will save his life, shall lose it: and he that shall lose his life for my sake, shall find it.

For what doth it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, and suffer the loss of his own soul? Or what exchange shall a man give for his soul?

For the Son of man shall come in the glory of his Father with his angels: and then will he render to every man according to his works. Amen I say to you, there are some of them that stand here, that shall not taste death, till they see the Son of man coming in his kingdom.

❤ Reflection

This teaching lands right after Jesus tells his disciples, for the first time, that he's going to Jerusalem to suffer and die — and right after Peter tries to talk him out of it, only to be told, bluntly, "Get behind me, Satan." Then Jesus turns to all of them and lays out exactly what following him is actually going to cost: not comfort, but self-denial. Not a safer version of their old lives, but a cross.

The line at the center of this passage isn't just a nice-sounding paradox — it's a real claim about how life actually works. "Whoever wishes to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake will find it." Jesus isn't describing a trade, where you give something up here and get a different thing back over there. He's saying the very thing you're white-knuckling — your comfort, your control, your own plan for how your life is supposed to go — is the thing standing between you and actually finding it. Loosening that grip isn't how you lose your life. It's how you get it back.

Picture this: a decision you keep avoiding because saying yes to it would mean giving up control — a career move, a relationship, an honest conversation, a change God's been nudging you toward for a while. The fear underneath is almost always the same: if I let go of my grip here, I'll lose something. Jesus's paradox says the opposite might be true — the tight grip itself might be what's costing you the life you actually want.

What are you gripping tightly right now out of fear of losing it — and what might it look like to actually loosen that grip and follow, trusting that this is how you find real life, not lose it?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 618

The Catechism describes the Cross as Christ's unique sacrifice, and notes that he calls his disciples to take up their own cross and follow him, since he left us "an example that we should follow in his steps." Denying yourself isn't a punishment tacked onto discipleship — it's what following him actually looks like.

Read CCC 618 →
CCC 2015

"The way of perfection passes by way of the Cross. There is no holiness without renunciation and spiritual battle." The Catechism doesn't present self-denial as an unfortunate side effect of faith — it's described as the actual path holiness travels.

Read CCC 2015 →
CCC 2544

On detachment from riches and self-sufficiency as necessary for entering fully into the Kingdom — directly connected to Jesus's question here about gaining the whole world and forfeiting your life. What you cling to for security can become exactly what keeps you from the life he's offering.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, show me what I'm gripping too tightly out of fear, and give me the courage to loosen my hold — trusting that losing my life for your sake is not a loss at all, but the way I actually find it. Amen.

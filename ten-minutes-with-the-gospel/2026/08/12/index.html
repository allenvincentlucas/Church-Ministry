Ten minutes with today's Gospel

Between You and Him Alone

Not a group chat. Not silence. Jesus gives a specific, private first step for conflict — go to the person directly, before anyone else knows.

Matthew 18:15-20

📖 Today's Gospel

Then Jesus said to his disciples: But if thy brother shall offend against thee, go, and rebuke him between thee and him alone. If he shall hear thee, thou shalt gain thy brother. And if he will not hear thee, take with thee one or two more: that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand.

And if he will not hear them: tell the church. And if he will not hear the church, let him be to thee as the heathen and publican. Amen I say to you, whatsoever you shall bind upon earth, shall be bound also in heaven; and whatsoever you shall loose upon earth, shall be loosed also in heaven.

Again I say to you, that if two of you shall consent upon earth, concerning any thing whatsoever they shall ask, it shall be done to them by my Father who is in heaven. For where there are two or three gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.

❤ Reflection

Jesus doesn't tell the wronged party to post about it, vent to a friend first, or simply let it go and pretend everything is fine. He gives a specific, deliberate order: go to the person directly, privately, first. Most conflict in community life skips this step entirely — either avoiding it altogether or going straight to a group before ever speaking one-on-one with the person involved.

Each step in this process exists for a reason. Private correction protects the other person's dignity and gives them the best chance to actually hear what's being said without an audience to defend against. Only if that fails does the circle widen — witnesses, then the community — and even that escalation is aimed at reconciliation, not humiliation. The passage ends with a promise: "where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them" — spoken right after describing exactly this hard, relational work. Christ's presence is promised in the middle of pursuing reconciliation, not only in comfortable, easy unity.

Picture this: someone on your team, in your family, or at church has hurt or frustrated you, and instead of talking to them, you've talked about them — to everyone except the one person who actually needs to hear it. Or maybe you've gone the opposite direction, confronting too publicly, too fast, skipping the private step entirely. This Gospel asks for the harder, quieter road: them, alone, first.

Is there someone you need to go to directly and privately, first — instead of talking about them to everyone except them?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 1443-1445

On the Church's ministry of reconciliation and the process Jesus lays out here for correcting a brother — the Catechism connects this passage directly to how the Church exercises the authority to "bind and loose," always aimed at restoring someone to communion, not excluding them permanently.

CCC 2478

On the duty of charitable interpretation and correction — presuming the best about someone until proven otherwise, and when correction is needed, offering it "in a spirit of gentleness" aimed at their good, not at winning an argument.

CCC 2842

On the connection between receiving God's forgiveness and extending it to others — the same relational honesty Jesus asks for in fraternal correction is rooted in this deeper truth: how we treat each other reflects how we've actually received mercy ourselves.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, give me the courage to go directly to the person I need to speak with, privately and honestly, instead of avoiding them or talking about them to everyone else. Make me quick to seek reconciliation and slow to give up on anyone. Amen.

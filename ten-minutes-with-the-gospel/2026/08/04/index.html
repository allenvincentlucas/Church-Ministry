He Comes in the Fourth Watch

The disciples spent hours battling the storm alone before Jesus ever showed up — and when he finally did, in the deepest part of the night, their first instinct was to be terrified of him too.

Matthew 14:22-36

📖 Today's Gospel

And forthwith Jesus obliged his disciples to go up into the boat, and to go before him over the water, till he dismissed the people. And having dismissed the multitude, he went into a mountain alone to pray. And when it was evening, he was there alone.

But the boat in the midst of the sea was tossed with the waves: for the wind was contrary. And in the fourth watch of the night, he came to them walking upon the sea. And they seeing him walk upon the sea, were troubled, saying: It is an apparition. And they cried out for fear. And immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying: Be of good heart: it is I, fear ye not.

And Peter making answer, said: Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters. And he said: Come. And Peter going down out of the boat, walked upon the water to come to Jesus. But seeing the wind strong, he was afraid: and when he began to sink, he cried out, saying: Lord, save me. And immediately Jesus stretching forth his hand took hold of him, and said to him: O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?

And when they were come up into the boat, the wind ceased. And they that were in the boat came and adored him, saying: Indeed thou art the Son of God.

And having passed the water, they came into the country of Genesar. And when the men of that place had knowledge of him, they sent into all that country, and brought to him all that were diseased. And they besought him that they might touch but the hem of his garment. And as many as touched, were made whole.

❤ Reflection

Look at the timeline here. The storm starts sometime after evening. Jesus doesn't come until "the fourth watch of the night" — the last stretch before dawn, somewhere around 3 to 6 a.m. That means hours of rowing against wind and waves, alone, before any help arrived. He wasn't unaware of the storm. He'd sent the disciples ahead on purpose and gone up the mountain to pray. His absence during the worst of it wasn't neglect — it was a timing that wasn't obvious from inside the boat.

And when he finally does come, the first reaction isn't relief. It's more fear. "It is an apparition," they cry out. They were so gripped by the crisis that when rescue actually showed up, they couldn't recognize it as rescue — it just looked like one more frightening thing in an already frightening night. It takes Jesus's own voice, naming himself, to reorient them: "Be of good heart: it is I, fear ye not."

Picture this: you've been praying through a long, hard stretch — a job search, an illness, a grief that won't lift — and by the time anything actually changes, you're so exhausted and braced for bad news that when help does show up, a phone call, an opening, a person, your first response is suspicion instead of relief. Crisis-mode doesn't switch off the instant the crisis ends.

Has fear or exhaustion ever made it hard for you to recognize help when it was already arriving — and what would it take to hear "it is I, fear not" in your own situation right now?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 207, 211

When Jesus says "it is I," he echoes the divine name revealed to Moses — "I AM" — which the Catechism describes as God revealing his faithfulness across time: present in the past, and promised for the future. The disciples weren't just seeing a man walk on water; they were hearing God name himself again.

Read CCC 207-211 →
CCC 303

Scripture is described as unanimous that God's providence is "concrete and immediate" — attentive to the smallest things as much as the great events of history. That includes a single overnight crossing on a small, frightened boat.

Read CCC 303 →
CCC 322

Christ invites "filial trust" in the Father's providence, and Peter the apostle is quoted directly: "Cast all your anxieties on him, for he cares about you." That's not abstract comfort — it's the same Peter who was in that boat.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, when I'm in the fourth watch of my own night, waiting longer than I want to for you to show up, help me recognize your voice when you come — instead of mistaking rescue for one more thing to fear. Amen.

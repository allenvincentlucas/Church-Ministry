Compassion From an Empty Tank

Jesus is grieving and exhausted, trying to get away and be alone — and still, when the crowd finds him anyway, he has compassion, heals their sick, and feeds them from almost nothing.

Matthew 14:13-21

📖 Today's Gospel

Which when Jesus had heard, he retired from thence by boat, into a desert place apart, and the multitudes having heard of it, followed him on foot out of the cities. And he coming forth saw a great multitude, and had compassion on them, and healed their sick.

And when it was evening, his disciples came to him, saying: This is a desert place, and the hour is now past: send away the multitudes, that going into the towns, they may buy themselves victuals. But Jesus said to them, They have no need to go: give you them to eat.

They answered him: We have not here, but five loaves, and two fishes. He said to them: Bring them hither to me. And when he had commanded the multitudes to sit down upon the grass, he took the five loaves and the two fishes, and looking up to heaven, he blessed, and brake, and gave the loaves to his disciples, and the disciples to the multitudes.

And they did all eat, and were filled. And they took up what remained, twelve full baskets of fragments. And the number of them that did eat, was five thousand men, besides women and children.

❤ Reflection

Notice where this story starts: Jesus has just heard that John the Baptist — his cousin, the one who baptized him — has been executed. He tries to get away by boat, alone, to grieve. That's a completely reasonable thing to need. But the crowds hear where he's headed and beat him there on foot. Matthew doesn't record any irritation from Jesus. Instead: "he had compassion on them, and healed their sick." He came looking for space to receive, and the first thing he does is give.

By evening the disciples offer a sensible plan: send everyone into town to buy their own food. Jesus refuses — "give you them to eat" — and asks what they actually have. Not what they wish they had. Five loaves, two fish, nowhere near enough for a crowd this size. He doesn't wait for abundance. He takes the small, insufficient thing already in their hands and multiplies it from there.

Picture this: you've had a hard week — maybe grief, maybe just exhaustion — and then your kids, or a friend, or someone at work still needs you tonight, right on schedule, whether you have anything left or not. The instinct is to feel like you have nothing to offer. But Jesus doesn't ask his disciples for a full pantry. He asks for what's actually there, however small, and works with that.

Where are you being asked to give from what feels like an empty tank right now — and what's the small, real thing already in your hands that God might do something with?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 1335

The Catechism names this exact miracle by name: the multiplication of the loaves, where the Lord blesses, breaks, and distributes bread through his disciples, is described as a preview of the "superabundance" the Eucharist would become.

Read CCC 1335 →
CCC 2447

Feeding the hungry is named as one of the corporal works of mercy — not an optional extra to faith, but one of its most basic, concrete expressions. Jesus doesn't just teach about compassion here; he demonstrates what acting on it actually looks like.

Read CCC 2447 →
CCC 2448

Human misery — including hunger, exhaustion, and grief — is described as what "elicited the compassion of Christ the Savior, who willingly took it upon himself." That's exactly the order of events in today's Gospel: his own grief doesn't close him off from theirs.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, when I feel like I have nothing left to give, remind me that you don't need abundance — you multiply whatever is actually in my hands. Give me the compassion you had for that crowd, even on the days I came looking for rest instead. Amen.

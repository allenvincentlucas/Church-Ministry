The Only One Who Sank Got Out of the Boat

Eleven disciples stayed dry in the boat and did nothing. Peter got out, sank halfway there, and was still the one Jesus caught by the hand.

Matthew 14:22-33

📖 Today's Gospel

And when he had dismissed the multitude, he went into a mountain alone to pray. And when it was evening, he was there alone. But the boat in the midst of the sea was tossed with the waves: for the wind was contrary. And in the fourth watch of the night, he came to them walking upon the sea.

And they seeing him walk upon the sea, were troubled, saying: It is an apparition. And they cried out for fear. And immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying: Be of good heart: it is I, fear ye not. And Peter making answer, said: Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters. And he said: Come. And Peter going down out of the boat, walked upon the water to come to Jesus.

But seeing the wind strong, he was afraid: and when he began to sink, he cried out, saying: Lord, save me. And immediately Jesus stretching forth his hand, took hold of him, and said to him: O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt? And when they were come up into the boat, the wind ceased. And they that were in the boat came and adored him, saying: Indeed thou art the Son of God.

❤ Reflection

It's easy to read Peter's sinking as the low point of this story — proof his faith wasn't quite enough. But look at what actually happens structurally first: eleven other disciples stay in the boat the entire time, watching a man walk on open water, and do nothing but panic and call it a ghost. Only one of them asks to come. Only one gets out. Whatever else is true about the sinking, it only happened to the one faith in that boat that was actually tested against something impossible.

Jesus's question afterward — "why did you doubt?" — isn't "why did you try." It's specific to the exact moment Peter's attention moved from Jesus to the wind. And notice what happens next: it isn't just Peter who ends up amazed. The whole boat worships, saying "Truly you are the Son of God" — something none of them said after merely watching from a safe distance. Peter's imperfect, half-sunk attempt produces more faith in the group than if he'd simply stayed dry with everyone else.

Picture this: a step of faith you've been putting off — a hard conversation, a ministry commitment, an honest prayer you're afraid to actually pray — because you're not sure you'll follow through without stumbling. This Gospel doesn't promise you won't sink partway. It shows that getting out of the boat, even imperfectly, is still worth infinitely more than staying safely seated and dry.

Where have you stayed in the boat out of fear of sinking — and what might happen, even in a partial failure, if you got out anyway?

✝ Catechism Cross-References

CCC 2610

Jesus's teaching on prayer and faith is tied to "the boldness of faith" — real trust that acts, not caution that only watches. Peter's request to come out onto the water is exactly that kind of bold, active faith, even though it falters partway.

Read CCC 2610 →
CCC 153

Faith is described as a gift of grace that still requires the free response and cooperation of the person — Peter both receives Jesus's invitation ("Come") and has to actually step out of the boat. Neither the grace nor the willingness to act is optional; both are present in this scene.

Read CCC 153 →
CCC 2088-2089

On doubt against faith — the Catechism distinguishes voluntary doubt from the ordinary hesitations and difficulties every believer faces. Peter's doubt here isn't treated as disqualifying; Jesus catches him instead of leaving him to sink, which is itself a picture of how God meets real, struggling faith.

🙏 Closing Prayer

Lord, when you call me out of the boat, give me the courage to actually step out, even knowing I might falter halfway there. And when I do start to sink, let my first instinct be Peter's: "Lord, save me" — trusting that your hand reaches out immediately. Amen.

Lesson 1 · 10 min

Before you open Zoom

Get everything ready so the service doesn't start with a scramble.

  • Arrive at least 20 minutes early — technical hiccups always take longer to fix than you expect
  • Internet is steady — a quick way to check: open any video site and see if it plays smoothly without pausing
  • Laptop charger plugged in — a dead battery mid-service is one of the most avoidable problems
  • FreeShow open ahead of time, with today's slides confirmed loaded
  • Unneeded apps and browser tabs closed — fewer things running means a smoother Zoom experience
  • A backup plan in mind — a phone hotspot or a second device, in case the main connection drops
If you only do one thing before service: test your internet and charge your laptop. Everything else can be fixed live — a dead laptop can't.

Lesson 2 · 10 min

Starting the meeting

Understand the difference between joining and starting, so you have full control from the first minute.

  • Signed in to the Zoom app with the church's host account — not just any account
  • Today's scheduled service found on the Zoom home screen
  • Clicked Start, not Join — starting the meeting makes you the host, which gives you the controls this training covers
  • Camera and microphone on, and checked to look and sound right before anyone joins
  • Confirmed with your AV partner that sound is clear and FreeShow is ready to share
  • Meeting started about 10–15 minutes early so early arrivals have somewhere to land
If you ever join a meeting instead of starting it, you won't have host controls like muting others or sharing your screen properly. When in doubt, check you clicked Start.

Lesson 3 · 15 min

Knowing your screen

What each button on the Zoom toolbar actually does, in plain terms.

ButtonWhat it does
Mute / UnmuteTurns your own microphone on or off
Stop Video / Start VideoTurns your own camera on or off
ParticipantsOpens the list of everyone in the meeting — where you admit, mute, or remove people
ChatOpens the text chat box, where people type messages and questions
Share ScreenLets you show something from your computer (like FreeShow) to everyone in the meeting
RecordStarts or stops recording the service — only used when asked (see Lesson 9 in the next topic)
EndThe red button on the far right — how the service officially closes (see Lesson 11)
You don't need to memorize every corner of Zoom. These seven buttons cover almost everything you'll touch during a service.

Lesson 4 · 10 min

Letting people in

Move people from the waiting room into the service smoothly.

  • Open Participants — if the Waiting Room is on, you'll see a separate list of people waiting to be let in
  • Click Admit next to a name, or Admit All to let everyone in at once
  • Check the waiting room every minute or two during the first 10–15 minutes — this is when most people arrive
  • If a name looks unusual or spam-like, hold off admitting and flag it to your AV partner or coordinator
  • Once service has properly started, you can slow down — most latecomers can be admitted quietly without disrupting anything
It's okay to admit people in small batches rather than all at once — it gives you a moment to notice anything unusual before it's in the room.

Lesson 5 · 10 min

Muting made simple

Keep the service audio clean without cutting anyone off from participating.

  • Open Participants — each person has a microphone icon next to their name
  • Click the microphone icon next to a name to mute just that person
  • Use Mute All at the bottom of the Participants list to mute everyone at once
  • Unmute someone individually if they need to speak, or ask them to unmute themselves
  • Remember to unmute yourself before you speak — the single most common on-air mistake
Default to keeping the congregation muted during the message to avoid background noise. It's easy to unmute someone the moment they need to speak.

Lesson 6 · 15 min

Sharing FreeShow on screen

Get the right FreeShow window on everyone's screen — and know how to take it down cleanly.

  • Click Share Screen in the Zoom toolbar
  • Choose the FreeShow output window — the one showing the actual slide, not the editor with all the buttons and menus
  • Click Share to confirm
  • Double check with your AV partner that what's showing on Zoom matches what's meant to be on screen
  • Click Stop Share when it's no longer needed, to return to normal view
The most common slide mistake is sharing the FreeShow editor window by accident, so the congregation sees your menus and buttons instead of the slide. Always pick the output window.

Lesson 7 · 10 min

Saying hello in chat

Make people feel welcomed and looked after through the chat box.

  • Greet people warmly as they join — a short "Welcome, glad you're here!" goes a long way
  • Keep an eye on chat throughout the service for simple questions like "can you hear us?" or "what's today's reading?"
  • Answer what you comfortably can yourself, with a warm, unhurried tone — this is still part of the service
  • Pass technical problems (no sound, frozen screen) to your AV partner, or check the Troubleshooting topic
  • Avoid getting pulled into long conversations in chat — a brief, kind reply is enough; you still need to watch the room
A participant messages "we can't hear anything!" during the sermon. What's the most likely fix?
The host forgetting to unmute themselves before speaking is the single most common on-air audio complaint. Check your own mic status first before assuming a wider issue.

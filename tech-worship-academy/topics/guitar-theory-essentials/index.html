Day 22 · 20 min

The major scale in one shape: a CAGED primer

The CAGED system shows how the same five open chord shapes (C, A, G, E, D) repeat all the way up the neck in different positions. Understanding just one shape's worth of major scale gives you a map for melody, not just chords.

  • Play a G major scale starting from your open G chord shape, one note at a time
  • Notice how the scale notes relate to the chord shape you already know
You don't need to master all five CAGED shapes right away — recognizing that chord shapes and scale shapes are connected is the goal for this week.
Day 23 · 15 min

How chords are built from scales: triads explained

A triad is the simplest chord: three notes stacked in thirds from the scale. Every open chord you've learned so far is a triad (or an extended version of one) — seeing the pattern helps you understand why some chords sound "related."

Day 24 · 15 min

The 1-4-5 progression behind hundreds of songs

Numbering chords by their position in the scale (1, 4, 5, and so on) lets you describe a progression independent of key — and once you know it, you'll start hearing it everywhere, including in songs you already play.

ProgressionChords in GYou already know this from
1–4–5G – C – DClassic rock, folk, and countless worship songs
1–4–5 in AA – D – EAll Creation Sing (Week 3) — same math, different key
1–5–6–4G – D – Em – CThe "four chord song" pattern heard everywhere
In the key of G, what are the 1-4-5 chords?
G is the root (1), C is a fourth up the scale (4), and D is a fifth up the scale (5) — this is the most common chord progression in popular music, and the same shape you saw in A with All Creation Sing.
Day 25 · 15 min

Capo basics: transposing without new shapes

A capo lets you play the same familiar open-chord shapes in a different key, which is exactly why No Greater Love (from Week 3) is written in F but played with a capo at fret 3 using Dm, Bb, F, and C shapes.

For the full deep dive — choosing a position, transposing by ear, and more worship song examples — see the Guitar Capo School topic.
Day 26 · 15 min

Intro to the minor pentatonic scale for soloing

The minor pentatonic is usually the first scale guitarists learn for playing melodies and solos, not just chords. It's a five-note "box" shape that's forgiving to improvise with — useful for instrumental intros or interludes in a worship set.

Day 27 · 15 min

Ear training: recognizing major vs. minor by sound

Major chords tend to sound bright or resolved; minor chords tend to sound darker or more tense. Training your ear to tell them apart quickly helps you follow along in rehearsal even before you see a chart.

  • Play G (major) then Em (minor) back to back, and describe the difference in your own words
  • Have someone play a random mix of major and minor chords while you guess, eyes closed

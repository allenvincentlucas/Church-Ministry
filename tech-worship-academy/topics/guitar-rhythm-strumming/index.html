Day 8 · 15 min

The down-up strumming pattern

Most strumming patterns are built from just two motions: a downstroke and an upstroke. Keeping your strumming hand moving in a constant down-up motion — even over strings you don't strike — is what keeps you in time.

  • Practice a steady down-down-down-down pattern on a G chord first, no upstrokes
  • Add upstrokes on the off-beats: down-up-down-up
  • Keep your arm moving continuously, even when you plan to "skip" a strum

Day 9 · 15 min

The "boom-chick" pattern

Common in folk, country, and many worship ballads, this pattern alternates a bass-note pluck with a strum — giving a song a driving, walking feel even at a slow tempo.

  • On a G chord, pluck the low E string alone ("boom"), then strum the rest ("chick")
  • Alternate which bass string you hit based on the chord (usually the root note)
  • Try it slowly on G, then C, then D
Day 10 · 15 min

Reading strumming pattern notation

Chord charts often show a strumming pattern as a row of D's and U's (down/up), sometimes with an "x" for a muted strum. Learning to read this quickly means you can pick up any chart the team hands you.

SymbolMeaning
DDownstroke
UUpstroke
xMuted/percussive strum (see Day 11)
( )A strum you skip, but your arm still moves through
D U (D) U D U D U is one of the most common patterns in modern worship music — the second beat is a "ghost" strum you skip but still swing through.
Day 11 · 15 min

Muting strings for a percussive strum

Resting the side of your strumming-hand palm lightly on the strings near the bridge lets you strum a rhythmic "chunk" sound instead of a ringing chord — useful for building energy without adding volume.

  • Rest your palm lightly on the strings near the bridge while strumming
  • Practice switching between a full ringing strum and a muted "chunk" strum on command
  • Try muting just the "and" beats in a D U D U pattern for a syncopated feel
Day 12 · 15 min

Practicing with a metronome, without losing the feel

A metronome (or a click track, which your team may already use) is only useful if it doesn't make your playing feel robotic. The goal is to internalize the pulse, not stare at a clock.

  • Set a metronome to a slow tempo (60–70 bpm) and strum quarter notes only
  • Once steady, add your down-up pattern from Day 8 over the same click
  • Try setting the click to only beats 2 and 4 (a backbeat) for a more musical challenge
If a strumming pattern feels rushed or uneven, slow the whole song down first — speed is the last thing you add, not the first.

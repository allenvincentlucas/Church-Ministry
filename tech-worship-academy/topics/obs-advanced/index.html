Lesson 1 · 25 min

FreeShow lyrics over NDI

FreeShow sends slides and lyrics to OBS over the network using NDI, rather than a heavy screen-capture. To receive that feed, OBS needs the DistroAV plugin (formerly obs-ndi) installed, plus the NDI 6 Runtime.

  • OBS Studio is version 31.0 or newer
  • DistroAV plugin installed from its official GitHub releases page
  • NDI 6 Runtime installed and computer rebooted

In FreeShow, build a style with the background disabled so it outputs transparent — a checkerboard pattern in the output thumbnail confirms transparency is working. In OBS, add an NDI Source in your worship scene, select the FreeShow output, and place it above your camera source in the Sources list so lyrics render on top of the picture instead of under it.

Lesson 2 · 20 min

Multi-camera switching

A second angle keeps a stream from feeling static. Add each camera as its own Video Capture Device source, either in separate scenes (cut between them like the pre-built service scenes) or layered in one scene as picture-in-picture. Give each camera's "switch to scene" or "show source" its own hotkey — the same way you set hotkeys for service scenes — so switching angles doesn't mean hunting through menus live.

Bandwidth note: multiple USB cameras can compete for the same USB controller's bandwidth. If a second camera looks choppy, try a different USB port or a powered USB hub before assuming the camera itself is the problem.

Phone as a camera

A spare smartphone can become a wireless second angle without buying a capture card — using the same NDI protocol FreeShow already uses. Install an NDI camera app (such as NDI HX Camera) on the phone, connect it to the same Wi-Fi network as the booth computer, and it appears as an NDI Source in OBS just like the FreeShow feed does.

This works best on a 5GHz Wi-Fi network, or wired if possible — a crowded 2.4GHz network is the most common cause of a laggy phone camera.

Lesson 4 · 20 min

Bridging to Zoom

Video: click Start Virtual Camera in OBS's Controls dock, then in Zoom go to Settings → Video and choose OBS Virtual Camera.

Audio needs a virtual cable, since Zoom can't pick up OBS's program audio directly. Install VB-Audio Virtual Cable, set OBS's Settings → Audio → Advanced → Monitoring Device to the Cable Input, and set each active channel's Advanced Audio Properties to Monitor and Output. In Zoom, set the microphone to the Cable Output and enable Original Sound for Musicians so Zoom's noise filter doesn't suppress the worship music.

Lesson 6 · 10 min

Copyright & streaming licensing

This one isn't a software setting, but it's easy for a tech volunteer to never hear about it until a video gets flagged. Streaming a service that includes copyrighted worship music — even performed live by your own musicians — needs a licensing agreement on top of any in-building copyright license your church already holds.

A CCLI Streaming License (or an equivalent like OneLicense) is what specifically authorizes including live-performed worship music in a stream or webcast. It's separate from a standard Church Copyright License, which only covers printing and displaying lyrics in the room.

A few things worth knowing as the person running the stream:

  • I know whether our church holds a current streaming license
  • I know it only covers songs performed live, not pre-recorded tracks or videos
  • I know who to ask before streaming a pre-recorded video or music track

This is a general orientation, not legal advice — check your church's actual license and its current terms directly with the licensor (e.g. ccli.com) before streaming anything you're unsure about.

Lesson 7 · 10 min

Closed captioning

Captions make the stream usable for viewers who are hard of hearing or watching without sound. The easiest option needs no extra software: on YouTube, when you go live, open Stream settings → Additional settings → Closed captions and choose Automatic captions. Facebook Live has a similar automatic-caption toggle under its live viewing settings.

Automatic captions are speech-recognition based — they'll misfire on unusual names, overlapping speech, or heavy background music, so treat them as a baseline rather than something to rely on word-for-word.

If you need captions independent of the platform (for example, embedding the stream on the church's own site), a separate tool such as Web Captioner can generate a caption overlay that OBS displays as a browser source.

A remote musician joining over Zoom says their instrument sounds muffled and thin. What's most likely to fix it?
Zoom's default noise suppression is tuned for speech and can flatten music. "Original Sound for Musicians" turns that processing off so instruments and full-range audio pass through cleanly.
  • I can bring FreeShow lyrics onto a live camera feed without help
  • I can switch between at least two camera angles smoothly
  • I can bridge audio and video into a Zoom or StreamYard session
  • I know our church's streaming license terms and our captioning setup
Once all four are checked, you're ready to run an integrated, multi-platform service alongside a more experienced volunteer nearby.

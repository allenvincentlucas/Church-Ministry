Lesson 1 · 15 min

Overview & unboxing

The L12next is a 12-channel digital mixer that also records to an SD card and streams over Bluetooth/USB — it's the one box doing front-of-house sound, a multitrack recorder, and an audio interface at the same time. You'll usually find it already mounted at the booth; you rarely unbox it yourself, but knowing its layout matters more than knowing how it shipped.

The top row of knobs controls each channel's gain and EQ. The center touchscreen is where routing, effects, and recording live. The channel faders along the bottom are what you'll touch most during a service — they control how loud each mic or instrument is in the main mix.

Before you touch anything live: find the master fader (far right) and the mute buttons for each channel. Those two controls solve 90% of "something's too loud" or "I can't hear the pastor" problems.

Lesson 2 · 20 min

Channel strip basics

Each channel strip has four things you'll adjust every week:

ControlWhat it doesWhen you touch it
GainSets how hot the incoming signal is before any processingOnce, during soundcheck
EQShapes tone — cuts muddiness or boosts clarityRarely, once dialed in
FaderSets that channel's volume in the mixConstantly, live
MuteInstantly silences the channelWhenever that mic isn't in use
Common mistake: riding the gain knob during service instead of the fader. Gain changes can introduce clipping or noise; the fader is the safe control to move live.

Lesson 3 · 20 min

Routing & monitor sends

"Routing" is just deciding where each channel's signal goes. The L12next lets you send any channel to the main mix (what the room hears) and, separately, to monitor mixes (what the stage hears) at a completely different volume balance. A vocalist might want more of themselves and less of the drums — that's a monitor send, and it never has to match the main mix.

On the touchscreen, tap a channel, then choose which mix bus you're editing before moving that channel's level. It's easy to accidentally edit the main mix when you meant to edit a monitor send — always check which bus is selected first.

Lesson 4 · 15 min

Recording to SD card

The L12next records every channel individually to an SD card, which is how most churches capture multitrack audio for later mixing or podcast/broadcast use. Insert a formatted SD card before service, confirm the record indicator is active, and never remove the card while a recording is running — it can corrupt the file.

  • SD card inserted and shows free space on screen
  • Recording started before the first live mic is unmuted
  • Recording stopped and confirmed saved after service ends

Lesson 5 · 15 min

Wireless app control

The companion app lets you adjust monitor mixes from a phone on stage instead of walking back to the booth every time a musician asks for more of themselves. Connect to the mixer's Wi-Fi network (check with your tech lead for the current network name), open the app, and you'll see the same channel strips as the touchscreen — just touch-friendly and mobile.

Musicians can often control their own monitor mix from their own phone once you've set this up for them — worth doing for anyone leading regularly.

Lesson 6 · 20 min

Running a live service

A typical Sunday flow: arrive early to power on and confirm every channel shows signal, run a quick soundcheck with anyone on stage, start the SD recording, then ride faders through the service — unmuting mics just before they're needed and muting them right after. Keep one hand near the master fader for any sudden feedback.

A vocalist is too quiet in the main room but says their monitor is fine. What do you adjust?
The main mix and monitor mixes are independent. Since the monitor is fine, the fix is the main-mix fader for that channel — not gain (which affects both) or the monitor send (which they said is fine).
Lesson 7 · 10 min

Troubleshooting & graduation checklist

Most live issues trace back to one of three things: a muted channel, a channel routed to the wrong bus, or a cable. Check the obvious before assuming the mixer is broken.

  • I can find and use the master fader and every channel mute without help
  • I can set up a basic monitor mix for a vocalist
  • I can start and safely stop an SD card recording
  • I've run at least one soundcheck under a mentor's supervision
Once all four are checked, you're ready to run a service solo alongside a more experienced volunteer nearby.

PP2-01 · 15 min

Progressive disclosure: animating bullet points

Sermon slides should reveal one point at a time as the pastor addresses it — showing the full outline early lets people read ahead instead of listening.

▶
How To Animate Bullet Points In PowerPoint (Easy Guide) Watch on YouTube ↗
  • Select the text box, open the Animations tab, apply "Appear" or "Fade"
  • Set each bullet's Start trigger to "On Click"
  • Test the click order in Slide Show view before service, not during it
  • Never expose the full outline on slide load — only progressive reveal
PP2-02 · 15 min

Sermon slide design

Sermon slides support the spoken message — they are not a transcript. Full paragraphs cause the congregation to read ahead and disengage from listening.

The 10-second / 140-character rule: a slide should be readable in about ten seconds, and stay under 140 characters of text.
RuleStandard
Line ceiling1 main point + max 4 sub-points
Word limit6–8 words per point
FocusSingle thought per slide — split complex sub-points across multiple slides
VisualsContextual (e.g. a map of Ephesus) — never decorative clip art or animation
PP2-03 · 10 min

Three-tier series branding

A consistent sermon-series identity is built from one master graphic, split into three purpose-built assets.

  • Title slide — vibrant branding, used for openers and transitions, no text overlay
  • Sermon content slide — darkened, desaturated version of the title art, for scripture and outline overlays
  • Background media slide — neutral texture for lyric overlays and announcements
Build all three from the same source graphic in your Slide Master so the series stays visually unified across every slide type.
PP2-04 · 10 min

Contrast & color for projection

Because a projector emits light rather than reflecting it, a bright white slide background floods the room and fatigues the eyes — the opposite of a printed page.

  • Prefer dark, desaturated backgrounds with light, high-contrast text
  • Add a subtle drop shadow or light stroke outline to text over dynamic backgrounds
  • Avoid color pairings that trigger visual vibration or accessibility failures: red/green, blue/orange, red/blue
Common mistake: a "clean-looking" white PowerPoint template made on a laptop screen can look washed-out and harsh once projected live.

PP2-06 · 15 min

CCLI copyright compliance

Projecting worship lyrics is governed by copyright law. The standard CCLI Church Copyright License covers most Sunday-service uses — but a few common mistakes still cause churches to fall out of compliance.

Required elementStandard
Song titleExact registered title — verify against CCLI SongSelect
Writer creditFull composer/lyricist names, not the performing artist
Copyright notice© symbol + year + publisher, as provided by CCLI
License numberYour church's CCLI license number — never the song's CCLI Song ID
Display frequencyAt least one slide per song (first or last) — never one master slide at the end of service
Note: the standard license does not cover streaming a live service online — that requires a separate CCLI Streaming License, and rehearsal use of commercial recordings requires a CCLI Rehearsal License.

Additional Resources: CCLI SongSelect for verifying exact titles and writer credits; remember the church's CCLI license number goes on the slide, never the CCLI Song ID; confirm whether your church holds a Streaming or Streaming Plus license before broadcasting worship music online.

FS-01 · 20 min

Interface & workspace layout

FreeShow is an open-source presentation engine built specifically for live church environments. Unlike simple slide software, it uses a layered system — text, foreground graphics, background media, and stage cues each sit on their own layer, so any one of them can be swapped or cleared without disturbing the rest.

When you launch it, the window splits into four working areas worth learning by name:

AreaWhat lives there
Project panelYour list of songs, scriptures, and media for the service
Slide grid editorThe actual slide content you're building or editing
The DrawerYour library — every song, media file, and template you own
Live monitorsExactly what's currently on the output screen, right now

FS-02 · 20 min

Content architecture: projects & shows

A "Show" in FreeShow is a single item — one song, one scripture reading, one announcement loop. A "Project" is the folder that holds everything for one service in the order you'll run it. Build your project the night before, in order, and Sunday morning becomes pressing next instead of searching.

Rename shows clearly the first time you create them — "Way Maker" not "Song 1" — future-you searching The Drawer at 8:55am on Sunday will thank you.

FS-03 · 20 min

The Drawer engine

The Drawer is your permanent library — every song you've ever built stays here, reusable across every future service. Search it by title instead of scrolling; it's built for fast recall mid-service when someone calls an audible on the setlist.

  • I can find any song in the Drawer by typing its title
  • I can drag a song from the Drawer into today's project

FS-04 · 20 min

Video & media routing

Background video, announcement loops, and full-screen media all route through the same output as your slides, which means transitions between a video and a lyric slide need to be clean and intentional. Preview media in the Live monitor before it's ever shown to the room, and know where the "clear output" control is so a bad cue never lingers on screen.

A background video is about to end and the next slide isn't cued yet. What's the safest move?
Clearing output first avoids a frozen or awkward frame being visible to the room while you find the next cue.

FS-05 · 15 min

Protocols & automation

FreeShow can talk to other booth gear — lighting, streaming software, stage displays — through automation triggers, so one cue can advance multiple systems at once. You don't need to build these yourself as a new volunteer, but knowing they exist explains why some cues seem to "do more" than just change a slide.

FS-06 · 15 min

Standard operating procedures & emergencies

Know these before your first live service:

  • Where the "clear output" / blank-screen control is, and that it's your panic button
  • How to re-cue a song from the top if the leader restarts a verse
  • Who to signal if FreeShow itself freezes or crashes mid-service
If the software crashes live: blank the output first (so the room sees a clean screen, not an error message), then restart — don't try to troubleshoot on the live output.

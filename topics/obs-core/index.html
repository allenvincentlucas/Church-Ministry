Lesson 1 · 15 min

Installing OBS & the Auto-Config Wizard

OBS Studio is free and open-source, downloaded from obsproject.com. It needs a Windows 10/11 or macOS 12+ machine with admin rights, since it needs low-level access to capture hardware and audio drivers.

The first time you open OBS, the Auto-Configuration Wizard checks your computer and network, then sets safe defaults for you. When it asks, choose Optimize for streaming, recording is secondary, and let it run its bandwidth test against your streaming platform.

SettingRecommended baseline
Base (canvas) resolution1920×1080
Framerate30 FPS
Video encoderHardware (NVENC / Apple Silicon VT) if available
If your church runs OBS on more than one booth computer, run the wizard fresh on each machine — hardware and network conditions differ per computer, so settings shouldn't just be copied over.

Lesson 2 · 15 min

Interface tour

The OBS window is a central preview canvas with five control docks along the bottom: the Scenes dock (your complete layout presets), the Sources dock (the individual pieces inside the active scene), the Audio Mixer (live peak meters and per-channel filter gears), the Scene Transitions dock, and the Controls dock — Start Streaming, Start Recording, Start Virtual Camera, and Settings.

Sources render top-to-bottom: whatever sits at the top of the Sources list draws over everything listed beneath it. This is how lower thirds end up "on top of" a camera feed later on.

Lesson 3 · 15 min

Building your first scene

Scenes are top-level layout containers; Sources are the individual elements inside them. To build a baseline "Worship Service" scene: add a new scene and name it 01_Main_Service, then add a Video Capture Device source named Main USB Camera. Select your camera from the dropdown, then apply Transform → Fit to Screen so the picture fills the canvas without black bars.

  • New scene created and named clearly
  • Camera source added and showing a live picture
  • Fit to Screen applied — no black bars, no stretching

Lesson 4 · 20 min

Audio setup & gain staging

Audio mistakes are the single biggest failure point in volunteer-run broadcasts. OBS captures your whole computer's system audio by default — that means notification pings and browser tabs can end up live. Go to Settings → Audio and set Desktop Audio to Disabled immediately.

Then set your real audio source under Settings → Audio → Mic/Auxiliary Audio — usually the USB output of the church's mixing console. Watch the Audio Mixer's peak meters during a soundcheck: spoken ministry should sit consistently in the yellow zone. Audio hitting red is clipping and needs to come down immediately.

Common mistake: leaving Desktop Audio enabled "just in case." It's the most common source of an accidental system-sound or feedback loop going live.

Lesson 5 · 15 min

Compressor & limiter: a safety net for clipping

Gain staging gets you close, but a sudden loud moment — someone bumping a mic, an enthusiastic "Amen!" — can still clip and distort. Two audio filters guard against this. Click the gear on your audio source in the Mixer, choose Filters, then add:

FilterWhat it doesWhere it sits
CompressorEvens out loud and quiet moments so the mix stays consistentEarlier in the filter chain
LimiterA hard ceiling that stops any spike from clipping, no matter whatAlways last in the chain
Think of the Limiter as the master fader's backup: gain staging and the compressor handle the normal range, the limiter catches anything that gets past both.

Lesson 6 · 15 min

Choosing a recording format

Whenever you're recording locally (see the next lesson for why you always should), OBS lets you pick the file format under Settings → Output → Recording Format. The two that matter for a volunteer team:

FormatWhat happens if OBS crashes mid-recordingUse it when
MKVFile stays playable — MKV is built to survive an interrupted writeYou want the safest default
MP4 (plain)The whole file can become unplayable if the recording didn't finish cleanlyYou need a file that opens anywhere without an extra step
Recommendation: record in MKV, then use OBS's built-in File → Remux Recordings afterward to create a matching MP4 — it takes seconds and loses no quality, and you keep the crash-safety of MKV either way.

Lesson 7 · 20 min

Service scenes & Studio Mode

Pre-building a scene for each phase of the service means no manual adjustments live. A typical Sunday scene list:

ScenePurpose
01_PreServiceHolding graphics and prelude music before the service starts
02_WorshipCamera feed with lyrics overlaid on top (see the Advanced topic for how)
03_PastoralFull-screen camera for preaching, communion, baptism
04_Sermon_SlidesSlide deck with a small picture-in-picture of the speaker
05_PostServiceA clean closing graphic with service times and links

Studio Mode splits the screen into a Preview (left, what you're staging) and Program (right, what's actually live) so you can set up the next scene without viewers seeing it. Cut for fast camera changes; use a short Fade for prayers and readings.

Going live

Enter your platform's Stream Key under Settings → Stream. Then, under Settings → Output (switch Output Mode to Advanced), set:

SettingRecommended value
Rate ControlCBR (Constant Bitrate)
Target BitratePer your platform's recommendation for your resolution/framerate
Keyframe Interval2 seconds (required for most platforms to ingest cleanly)
Encoder PresetQuality or Balanced
Your stream status indicator is showing a solid green light before you go live. What does that confirm?
The stream status light reflects connection health to the platform — it doesn't check your audio or your local backups. Those need separate checks.

Lesson 9 · 20 min

Hotkeys & backing up your setup

Clicking through a scene list live is slow and easy to fumble. Under Settings → Hotkeys, filter for "switch to scene" and assign each scene its own key — most booths use the number row (1, 2, 3...) matching scene order.

Just as important: your whole setup — scenes, sources, filters — lives in a Scene Collection file. Export it from Scene Collection → Export and keep a copy somewhere safe (a shared drive, not just the booth laptop). If that computer ever needs replacing or reinstalling, importing the file rebuilds your entire show in seconds instead of from scratch.

  • A hotkey is set for every scene you use on a Sunday
  • Scene Collection exported and saved outside the booth computer
  • Someone other than you knows where that backup file lives

Lesson 10 · 15 min

Pre-flight checklist & graduation

Run this every week, starting thirty minutes before the service:

TimeCheck
T-30Network cabling, power, and switches all connected
T-20Camera picture sharp; live mic test hits the yellow zone
T-10Any lyrics/graphics feed renders correctly in the OBS preview
T-5Short local test recording; stream status light solid green
T-0Select 01_PreService, press Start Streaming, confirm the feed live on the platform

If something breaks live: a black camera source usually means a dropped hardware handshake — right-click the source, Properties, Deactivate, wait a moment, then Activate again. Dropped frames usually mean network congestion — lower the bitrate and check nothing else is using the connection.

  • I can run the full pre-flight checklist without help
  • I can recover a frozen camera source live
  • I've exported and located our Scene Collection backup
  • I've run at least one full service under a mentor's supervision
Once all four are checked, you're ready to run a service solo alongside a more experienced volunteer nearby.
